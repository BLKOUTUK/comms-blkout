Ten reasons Instagram ain't the one
I like Instagram. Worth saying first — everything else here only makes sense if you know I'm not writing from outside it.
BLKOUT has spent this year trying to tell you about a picnic, a compass, a community we're building — and the feed keeps taking a cut. Reach we don't own. Attention we pay for twice. A platform deciding who hears from us, and what that costs.
Ten reasons, actually — not a purity test. An organisation with no reach is not principled. It is quiet. This is the honest account: what depending on somebody else's app costs a small Black queer organisation, and what we're building instead.

Ten years, and we're logging off
Making Space for Us
We posted our first tweet on 20 July 2014. Over ten years and forty thousand more, Twitter is where BLKOUT found each other before we had anywhere else to.
Our board decided this month: we're leaving. Not deleting — the account stays up, dormant, so nobody else can wear the name. A plaque on a building we no longer live in. Our last post explains why, and says a proper goodbye.

The noticeboard
A national listening exercise, hosted in our house. The National Conversation — a UK-wide project run with Oxford, asking what people value about community life — is running group sessions everywhere this autumn. We're hosting ours: Wednesday 23 September, 7–8pm, in BLKOUTHUB. Black queer men's answers rarely make it into a national picture. This is one hour to make sure ours do. Want in? rob@blkoutuk.com
BLKOUT in your neighbourhood. events.blkoutuk.com just got a local rebuild — gatherings, openings, a first list of QTIPOC places we vouch for, and now three borough doors of our own: Croydon, Lambeth and Enfield, each with its own events and history. Find yours, or tell us what's missing.
Room on the board — we're still looking for a treasurer. Someone curious about how money moves, who cares about this community. You don't need to already be an accountant — the board learns together, on purpose. Write to rob@blkoutuk.com.
New here? Ivor's Compass is still open — a digital journal through seven directions of a remarkable Black queer life. Claim yours.
